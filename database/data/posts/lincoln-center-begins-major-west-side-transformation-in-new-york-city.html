Lincoln Center for the Performing Arts officially began construction on a major redesign of its west side campus, adding new gardens, public spaces, and a new outdoor theater set to open in 2028. 

Lincoln Center for the Performing Arts officially broke ground this week on a major redevelopment project that will completely transform the west side of its iconic Manhattan campus.

The redesign will introduce new gardens, public gathering spaces, expanded greenery, improved pedestrian access, and a brand-new outdoor performance venue called The Baron Theater, designed to host around 2,000 guests. The project is expected to open in Summer 2028. 


The new venue will become part of the Stavros Niarchos Foundation (SNF) Gardens, a redesigned version of Damrosch Park that aims to create a more welcoming and accessible connection between Lincoln Center and the surrounding Upper West Side community. The project focuses heavily on accessibility, open public space, and year-round community use. 

One of the biggest changes includes removing the wall along Amsterdam Avenue that has historically separated the campus from neighboring communities. In its place, visitors will see open entrances, expanded sidewalks, seating areas, trees, gardens, lighting improvements, and accessible pathways designed for all visitors. 

Lincoln Center says the redesign was shaped through years of feedback from local residents, students, NYCHA communities, schools, and neighborhood organizations. Thousands of community members participated in the planning process. 

The project has already secured its full $335 million construction budget through support from private donors, foundations, corporations, and public funding from New York City and New York State. Two major $75 million gifts came from the Baron Family Foundation and the Stavros Niarchos Foundation. 

During the groundbreaking ceremony, Lincoln Center celebrated the cultural history of the neighborhood with live performances, poetry, jazz, ballet, and tributes to San Juan Hill, the historic community that existed in the area before Lincoln Center was built. Artists participating included jazz pianist Sean Mason, dancers from the School of American Ballet, New York City Ballet performers, and vocalist Catherine Russell. 

Earlier this spring, Lincoln Center also unveiled a large public mural titled The Future We Create along the construction fencing on Amsterdam Avenue. The artwork was created in collaboration with local residents and honors both historic figures and current members of the surrounding community. 

The redesign is being led by Hood Design Studio, WEISS/MANFREDI, and Moody Nolan as part of the larger Stavros Niarchos Foundation Lincoln Center West Initiative. Once completed, the project is expected to reshape the western edge of Lincoln Center into a more open and community-centered public space for New Yorkers and visitors alike.

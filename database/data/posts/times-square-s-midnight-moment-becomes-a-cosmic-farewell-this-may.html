Every night this May, Times Square transforms into a surreal cinematic departure into the unknown.

Times Square Arts is presenting artist Yael Bartana’s Farewell as the latest edition of Midnight Moment, the world’s largest digital public art exhibition. The installation will appear nightly through May 31 across more than 100 electronic billboards throughout Times Square from 11:57 p.m. until midnight.

The video work imagines humanity’s final departure from Earth aboard a fictional generation ship shaped like the Kabbalistic Tree of Life. Through choreographed movement and expressionist-inspired dance, Farewell explores themes of survival, displacement, ritual, and hope for renewal beyond Earth.

Moments before midnight, Times Square’s glowing screens become part of a symbolic countdown, inviting viewers into a dreamlike vision of humanity leaving the planet behind in search of new frontiers.

May’s Midnight Moment was guest-curated by Nato Thompson with Dreaming in Public, a curatorial group focused on bringing art into everyday public life.

Alongside the installation, The Jewish Museum will host a special screening and panel discussion on May 18 at 6:30 p.m., featuring Yael Bartana and curator Nato Thompson in conversation moderated by Darsie Alexander, Susan and Elihu Rose Chief Curator at the museum.


About Yael Bartana

Yael Bartana is an internationally recognized contemporary artist whose work explores national identity, collective memory, displacement, and political imagination through film, installations, photography, performance, and public monuments.

Her work has been exhibited globally, including at institutions such as:

  • MoMA PS1

  • Stedelijk Museum

  • Philadelphia Museum of Art

  • Jewish Museum Berlin

About Midnight Moment

Midnight Moment is presented monthly by Times Square Arts and transforms Times Square’s iconic billboards into a synchronized digital art exhibition each night just before midnight. The project has become one of New York City’s most unique public art experiences, blending contemporary visual art with the energy of the city’s most famous intersection.
